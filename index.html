Grants Pass v. Johnson 是 HSRP 必须钉死的房间里的大象

Grants Pass v. Johnson is the elephant HSRP must name

2024 年 6 月美国最高法院在 Grants Pass v. Johnson 一案中裁定：城市可以以刑事方式处罚无家者在公共场所露宿，即使该城市没有可用的庇护所床位。这个判决推翻了第九巡回上诉法院 Martin v. Boise 的保护，向全美城市开放了用刑事系统驱逐街头无家者的法律通道。任何关于美国 homelessness 的协议如果不直接处理这条，等于回避当下最强的结构暴力转向。

In June 2024, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled in Grants Pass v. Johnson that cities may criminally punish people experiencing homelessness for sleeping in public, even when no shelter beds are available. The ruling overturned Ninth Circuit protections under Martin v. Boise, opening the legal channel for cities nationwide to use the criminal system to displace unsheltered people. Any U.S. homelessness protocol that fails to address this directly avoids the strongest structural-violence pivot of the present moment.

METHOD VIOLENCE · COUNTING POLITICS

"PIT count" 在结构上漏掉了大多数儿童、青少年与家庭

The "PIT count" structurally misses most children, youth, and families

HUD 的单晚 PIT count 只统计庇护所、过渡住房与可见的街头无家者。学校系统数据（McKinney-Vento 法规下学校必须主动识别所有无家儿童）显示真实数字是 PIT 的 3–6 倍。沙发居住、汽车过夜、家庭挤住、被亲戚临时收留的"隐性无家"儿童与青少年——在 PIT 上几乎不存在。这是承认权问题：被计数权 = 政策可见性 = 资源分配。

HUD's single-night PIT count includes only people in shelters, transitional housing, or visibly on the street. School-system data (under McKinney-Vento, schools must proactively identify all homeless children) shows the true number is 3–6× the PIT figure. Couch-surfing, car-sleeping, doubled-up families, children temporarily taken in by relatives — these "hidden homeless" children and youth barely exist on PIT. This is a recognition-rights problem: the right to be counted = policy visibility = resource allocation.
